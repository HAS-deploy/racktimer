tempo training — strength-training glossary

What it means

Tempo training refers to controlling the speed of each phase of a lift — typically the lowering (eccentric), pause (isometric), and lifting (concentric) phases — rather than just moving the weight from point A to point B. It's usually written as a four-digit code (e.g., 3-1-2-0), where each number represents seconds spent in that phase. The goal is to make the muscle work harder for longer, increase time under tension, and develop body awareness and control.

Why it matters

Most casual lifters move as fast as they comfortably can, which often means momentum does a significant portion of the work. Deliberately slowing down the eccentric (lowering) phase, for instance, increases muscle damage and metabolic stress — two key drivers of hypertrophy. Tempo work also shows up in injury rehabilitation, where control and joint stability matter more than load. Powerlifters and Olympic weightlifters use it to iron out technical weak points in their lift. If you've ever been told to "lower the bar slowly" on a bench press, that's informal tempo coaching.

Example

A squat programmed as 4-1-1-0 means:

  • 4 seconds lowering into the squat
  • 1 second pause at the bottom
  • 1 second standing back up
  • 0 second pause at the top before the next rep

The same weight you can normally rep out becomes genuinely challenging when you spend 6+ seconds under load per repetition.

Common confusions

Tempo training vs. speed/dynamic effort work — These are opposites. Tempo training deliberately slows movement to increase time under tension; speed work (common in powerlifting) uses sub-maximal loads moved as fast as possible to develop rate of force development.

The eccentric vs. concentric phases — People sometimes lose track of which number applies to which phase, especially on pulling movements. On a deadlift, the concentric is pulling the bar up; the eccentric is lowering it back down. The code always starts from the beginning of the movement, which varies by exercise, so it's worth confirming how your coach or program defines the sequence.

Tempo as cardio pacing — In running, "tempo" means a comfortably hard sustained pace. In strength training it has nothing to do with cardiovascular effort and refers strictly to rep speed.
