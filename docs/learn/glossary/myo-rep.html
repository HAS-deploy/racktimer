myo-rep — strength-training glossary

What it means

A myo-rep is a rest-pause training technique designed to accumulate a high number of effective, near-failure reps in a short amount of time. You perform an activation set — typically 10–20 reps taken close to failure — then rest briefly (3–5 deep breaths, or roughly 20–30 seconds), and perform small "mini-sets" of 3–5 reps with the same weight, repeating until you can no longer hit the target mini-set rep count. The logic is that the muscle fibers recruited in those final hard reps of the activation set stay recruited through the short rest periods, meaning every subsequent mini-set is composed almost entirely of high-quality, growth-stimulating reps.

The technique was developed by Norwegian coach Børge Fagerli as a way to get the muscle-building stimulus of many hard sets without the total time cost. It's especially popular in programs where training efficiency matters or volume needs to be compressed.

Why it matters

Myo-reps come up most often in intermediate-to-advanced hypertrophy programs where you want to maximize effective reps per unit of time. They work best on isolation or machine exercises — cable flyes, lateral raises, leg press, leg curls — where fatigue is local and form breakdown under rest-pause conditions is minimal. They're less suited to heavy compound lifts like squats or deadlifts, where systemic fatigue and technique demands make very short rest periods risky.

If a program prescribes "3–5 myo-rep matches," that means completing the activation set and then hitting 3–5 mini-sets of the target rep count before the set is done.

Example

You load a cable row and perform 15 reps, stopping 1–2 reps shy of failure. You take 5 slow breaths, then do 5 more reps. Rest again, 5 more reps. Rest again — you only get 3 this time. Since you couldn't match your 5-rep target, the myo-rep set is complete. Total: 28 reps, the majority of which were high-effort, effective reps.

Common confusions

Myo-reps vs. drop sets: A drop set reduces weight to extend the set. Myo-reps keep the weight constant and use brief rests instead. The mechanisms are different — drop sets extend time under fatigue; myo-reps exploit sustained motor unit recruitment across short recovery windows.

Myo-reps vs. standard rest-pause: Rest-pause is a broad category. Myo-reps are a specific, structured version with a defined activation set, fixed breathing-based rest intervals, and a clear stopping rule (can't hit the mini-set target). Not every rest-pause set is a myo-rep set.

The activation set as the "hard part": Many lifters underestimate how close to failure the activation set needs to be. If it's too easy, the subsequent mini-sets won't recruit the high-threshold fibers that make the technique effective.
